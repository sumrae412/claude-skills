# Phase 1: Market Reality

## Goal

Produce a cold market read with TAM, saturation, buyer behavior, pricing
ceiling, channel viability, and kill criteria.

## Cover

- TAM and reasoning
- market maturity and saturation
- dominant players
- buyer sophistication
- purchase frequency
- viable acquisition channels
- adjacent-market proof (see Rules)
- CAC / CLV logic when possible
- **the number that decides it** (required — see Rules)
- strategic opportunity mode
- difficulty and verdict
- vitamin or painkiller (pick one, one-sentence justification)

## Rules

- Treat "vitamin vs painkiller" as a forced binary call. No "both" answers.
- Painkiller = customers are already cobbling together a workaround or paying for an inferior fix. Vitamin = nice-to-have, no active workaround.
- **Adjacent-market proof beats TAM projection.** Spend already flowing to a different buyer (enterprise, clinical, B2B) is observed evidence; a consumer TAM forecast is not. Name the incumbent channel and roughly what it captures. The useful shape is "the money is already here, just not flowing to this buyer" — which reframes the question from *is there a market* to *can this buyer be reached profitably*.
- **Name the number that decides it.** Every business has one variable that invalidates the plan if it lands wrong, regardless of how the rest scored. State it as a number with a threshold, say what crossing it changes, and name the cheapest experiment that settles it. Not a risk category — a number. Settle it before anything downstream gets built.
- **LTV carries a churn assumption or it is fiction.** Source the assumption from a comparable business; absent real data, show a conservative and a generous scenario rather than one figure. An unstated "customer stays five years" default is the most common way LTV becomes invented.
- **Every figure carries a source or the inline label `unverified`.** Applied before the output ships, not when someone asks.

## When primary research is warranted

If the gating risk is *"will a person pay recurring money for this"* and
desk research cannot settle it, load
[`../references/primary-research-playbook.md`](../references/primary-research-playbook.md)
— cheap screened-survey design, willingness-to-pay elicited as a number
and segmented by buying behaviour, and the reporting guardrails that keep
a small sample honest.

Skip it when the gating risk is technical feasibility, regulatory
approval, or enterprise sales-cycle length; the playbook's Fit section
says why it misleads there.

## Output

Market reality table plus kill criteria, adjacent-market proof, the number that decides it, vitamin/painkiller verdict, and strategist's note.
